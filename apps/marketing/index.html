How it works

You own the whole thing, start to finish

  1. 1

    Someone adds one line

    A developer pastes a single script tag into your product. Once. They never touch it again — not for your next tour, not for the one after that.

  2. 2

    You write it on the page

    Open your own product, hit a keyboard shortcut, and the editor appears on top of it. Click the button you want to point at. Type what you want to say.

  3. 3

    You check it like a customer

    Watch it play on staging exactly as it will run in production — same words, same styling, same behaviour. Send it to a colleague for approval if that's your process.

  4. 4

    You publish it

    What you approved is what goes live, unchanged. Wrong copy at 4pm on a Friday? Fix it, or put yesterday's version back, without asking anyone.

Questions

The things people actually ask

Using it

What is Lodariq?

Lodariq is in-product launch software — a digital adoption platform for B2B SaaS teams. A developer installs one script tag, once. After that, the person who owns the launch builds guided tours, walkthroughs and onboarding directly on the live product, without writing code or filing a ticket.

Do I need to know how to code?

No, and there's no hidden point where you suddenly do. You click the button you want to point at and type what you want to say. There's no CSS box, no selector to paste in, and no "advanced mode" that quietly requires a developer — those aren't things Lodariq can express at all.

How long does building one actually take?

Almost all of the time goes into deciding what to say — the building itself is pointing and typing. You work on the real product rather than describing it in another tool, so there's no step where you write instructions for a screen you can't see and find out later you got it wrong.

Can I control who sees it, and when?

Yes. Target by the attributes you already send about your users — plan, role, account age, anything you pass to Lodariq — and give it a start and end date so a time-limited announcement retires itself. You can also branch a tour so different people take different paths through it.

Does someone have to approve it before it goes live?

Only if you want that. There's a review and approval workflow if your team needs sign-off, and roles that separate who can draft from who can publish. If it's just you, you publish.

What if I publish something wrong?

Fix it and republish, or put the previous version back — rollback returns to a version that already existed rather than rebuilding it, so you get exactly what was live before. You don't need a developer, and you don't need to wait for a deploy.

How do I know whether it worked?

You see completions, drop-off by step, and dismissals — so you can tell the difference between "nobody finished" and "nobody wanted it in the first place." Staging and production are counted separately, so your own rehearsals don't end up in the number you report. Deeper analysis — funnels, cohorts, CSV export — is still being built.

What can I actually build today?

Tours, announcements, hotspots, checklists and surveys — all five run today, and all five are included on every plan. Tours cover most in-product messaging on their own: feature walkthroughs, onboarding, upgrade prompts and change notices, with rich content, audience branching, scheduling and review before release. The tour editor is the most mature of the five; the others are catching up.

What happens when we redesign the product?

Steps are anchored to what an element means — its role, its label, what's around it — the way you'd describe a button to a colleague, rather than to a code path that a restyle breaks. When something genuinely can't be found any more, the step is flagged for you to look at instead of silently pointing at nothing. We're not publishing a percentage for this until we can back one up with real measurement.

Can I use it on software we didn't build?

No. Lodariq needs a script tag on a product you control, so it can't be layered over third-party software your team doesn't own. If that's what you need, this is the wrong category of tool and we'd rather tell you now.

For your developer

What exactly gets added to our site?

One asynchronous script tag that renders nothing until something is published to that environment. The production runtime is framework-free — it never contains React or the editor, which live in a physically separate package, and the build fails in CI if a production module imports them.

Will it interfere with our styling?

No, in both directions. Cards render inside a shadow root, so no Lodariq CSS reaches your stylesheet and none of your CSS can break a card. Nothing a non-technical creator does can produce arbitrary CSS, JavaScript or raw HTML on your page — the document format has no way to represent them.

Does it work with React, Vue, Angular or server-rendered apps?

Yes. The runtime is framework-free and works anywhere a script tag runs. It resolves targets from the live DOM at playback time rather than from your component tree, so it doesn't care what rendered the page, and it waits for routes and lazy content to settle before attaching a step.

Can a tour trap or block our users?

No. Only the visible card intercepts input — everything outside its bounds stays clickable, so someone can keep using your product with a tour open. Cards are real dialogs with proper focus management and keyboard operability, targets are at least 44×44 pixels, reduced-motion settings are respected, and a WCAG AA contrast check blocks publishing content that would fail it.

How does something get from staging to production?

Publishing compiles the content into an immutable, content-addressed artifact. It gets verified on staging, and then that identical artifact is promoted to production — nothing is recompiled in between, so what was approved is byte-for-byte what ships. Rollback moves a pointer to a version that already existed, and the release history is append-only.

Can creators edit against our production site?

No. The editor doesn't exist on production builds at all — the code isn't shipped there. On an approved development or staging origin, a signed-in creator with the right role reveals it with a keyboard shortcut. The shortcut opens the door; it isn't what grants permission.

Security, data and buying

Can Lodariq see our customer data?

Only what you deliberately hand over. Lodariq has no access to your database and no mechanism to get it — it sees the traits you choose to pass in, events you choose to send, and whether the experiences it delivered were completed. Full-app session replay is permanently excluded from the product, and tenants are isolated with PostgreSQL row-level security.

Do you support SSO and role-based access?

Yes — SAML and OIDC single sign-on, and member, admin and owner roles that separate who can draft from who can publish. SSO isn't sold as a separate add-on tier.

How much does it cost?

There's no published price yet, because we haven't validated one with real teams. The model is settled: you're billed on people who were actually shown something that month rather than everyone who logged in, there's a real free tier, and going over never applies retroactively.
